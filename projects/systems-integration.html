The Challenge

Commerce teams rely on consistent inventory and product records, even when different platforms, stock locations or operational processes use different views of the same data.

My Approach

  • Structured inventory and product datasets to support reporting and reconciliation.
  • Applied normalization and exception-handling rules to improve consistency in operational reports.
  • Supported ERP-related item and inventory workflows and the exchange of data between business processes.
  • Contributed to commerce platform and system integration activities within broader operations responsibilities.

Results & Impact

The data and reporting work made inventory information easier to interpret and more dependable for everyday decisions. By organizing product records and applying consistent reconciliation rules, operational teams gained a clearer picture of the information behind storefront availability and internal reports.

  • Cleaner inventory views. Separating relevant stock categories and applying reporting rules helped staff distinguish available merchandise from stock that should not be counted the same way.
  • Fewer spreadsheet and SKU mistakes. Normalization of identifiers and structured product data reduced inconsistent formats, duplicate interpretation and avoidable manual cleanup during reporting.
  • Less time reconciling information. Consistent columns and defined adjustment rules made repeat reports easier to review and compare rather than requiring every employee to rediscover how values should be handled.
  • More reliable support for customers. Clearer product and availability data helped sales and customer-service teams work from more consistent information when answering stock and product questions.
  • Better cross-team handoffs. Structured reports provided a more usable bridge between e-commerce operations, warehouses and ERP-related processes, supporting quicker exception review and clearer decisions.

The practical result was a better operational information flow: fewer opportunities for copy-and-paste errors, less ambiguity around inventory records, and more time available for the exceptions that actually need human judgment.
